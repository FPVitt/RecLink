User guide

RecLink

Set up and use the camera bridge for your Betaflight quad: wiring, pairing, recording control, OSD and the web configurator.

Firmware 0.1.0 (pre-release)Betaflight 2025.12+GoPro HERO 11–13 · DJI Osmo Action 4/5/6, Osmo 360
Read first: pre-release status

This firmware (0.1.0) has been bench-tested on a custom ESP32-C3 board wired to a Betaflight flight controller and a GoPro HERO11 Black. It has not been flight tested, and some camera protocol values are still being verified against live captures. Treat every flight as a test flight. Do all setup and testing on the bench with propellers removed, and check every behaviour in this guide before you fly with it.

Passenger, not pilot

RecLink only reads from your flight controller and writes OSD text. It cannot arm, disarm, move motors, change PIDs or touch the radio link. That boundary is enforced in the firmware itself, not left to convention. If RecLink fails, the worst thing that can happen in flight is that the OSD text stops updating.

How to read this guide

Chapters 1–6 take you from a bare board to a paired camera and are meant to be followed in order. Chapters 7–12 explain each part of the web configurator. Chapters 13 and 14 cover day-to-day use and what to do when something is wrong. Screenshots show the real configurator on a phone.

Chapter 1

What RecLink is#

A small ESP32 board that sits between your flight controller and your action camera, so the camera is controlled by the aircraft and reports back on your OSD.

RecLink listens to the flight controller over a three-wire serial link and talks to the camera over Bluetooth. The Wi-Fi network exists only while the aircraft is disarmed, for configuration.

What it does#

  • Camera info on the OSD. Recording state, elapsed and remaining time, camera battery, SD space, resolution, frame rate and link status, as 22 tokens across four 16-character lines you design yourself.
  • Recording that follows the aircraft. Start and stop on arm, on a radio switch, or on a combination. Every command is checked against what the camera reports.
  • Camera settings from a switch. A 2–6 position switch or knob selects zones. On a GoPro each zone pushes its own resolution, frame rate, lens, HyperSmooth and Protune values. On a DJI each zone selects a camera mode.
  • Highlight tags from a momentary switch (GoPro HiLight, DJI Quick-Switch).
  • Up to four paired cameras, any mix of GoPro and DJI. At power-up it connects to the strongest one in range.
  • Clock sync from the flight controller's GPS time, so footage carries the right timestamp.
  • One web page for all configuration, plus firmware updates. No app, no cloud.

What it never does#

Hard limits
  • Never sends RC, GPS, clock, display or reboot commands to the flight controller, and never changes its configuration.
  • Never arms or disarms the aircraft.
  • Never runs Wi-Fi while armed. The access point is shut down within 100 ms of arming.
  • Never powers the camera, and never formats a card or deletes files from automation.
  • Never shows a value the camera has not confirmed. An unknown value renders blank, not 0 and not a guess.

Chapter 2

What you need#

A RecLink board, a free UART on your flight controller, and a camera that is on its own power.

Checklist

  • RecLink board (ESP32-C3) with its harness.
  • Flight controller running Betaflight 2025.12.0 or newer, with an OSD chip or HD OSD working.
  • A free UART on the FC with its TX, RX, 5 V and GND pads accessible.
  • A supported camera, charged, with an SD card.
  • A radio with at least one free AUX channel (two or three if you want record, zones and highlight).
  • A phone or laptop with Wi-Fi to run the configurator.
  • A bulk capacitor of at least 220 µF across the board's 5 V input, if your board does not already have one.
  • For firmware updates: a computer and the .bin file.

Controls and pins on the production board.

Know your two controls#

The board has one button and one status LED. They are all you need when the portal is closed, and every gesture works only while the board is running. Nothing is read at power-up.

Button gestures. Presses between 1 and 3 seconds do nothing.

Chapter 3

Wiring to the flight controller#

Four wires: two for serial data, two for power. The serial link is 3.3 V logic on both sides, so connect it directly with no level shifter.

Serial wires cross: the FC's TX goes to RecLink's RX, and RecLink's TX goes to the FC's RX.

  1. Pick a free UART on the flight controller and note its number. You will enable MSP on that UART in chapter 4.
  2. Connect the four wires as drawn. The board's TX line has a 470 Ω series resistor on the board. Do not add another.
  3. Add the capacitor if needed. Bluetooth and Wi-Fi transmit bursts draw about 350 mA peaks. On a long thin harness this can pull the supply down and reset the board. A 220 µF or larger low-ESR capacitor across the 5 V pads prevents that.
  4. Mount it close to the camera. Keep the board's antenna clear of carbon fibre and battery leads. Bluetooth range to the camera is the part that suffers from bad placement.
  5. Leave the camera's power alone. The camera runs from its own battery or BEC. RecLink never powers or biases it.
Crossed the wires? No need to resolder

If TX and RX end up swapped on a four-pin harness, you can fix it in the configurator: Network → Swap TX/RX pins (advanced mode), then reboot. See chapter 12.

Power

The board takes 5 V from the flight controller through its onboard regulator. Check that the pad you use is 5 V and not battery voltage before you connect. The board has a brown-out detector: a dip in supply resets it and it recovers on its own within about 300 ms, but the OSD text freezes for that moment.

Chapter 4

Betaflight setup#

Two changes in Betaflight Configurator: turn on MSP on the UART you wired, and make four custom message elements visible on the OSD.

Use the latest Betaflight Configurator

You need Betaflight firmware 2025.12.0 or newer on the flight controller, and the latest Betaflight Configurator on your computer. The screenshots in this guide were taken with Configurator 2026.6.2. Use the web app at app.betaflight.com (in Chrome or Edge) or install the newest desktop version. An older Configurator may not offer the Custom message elements.

Version check

Custom OSD messages exist only in Betaflight 2025.12.0 and newer (MSP API 1.47 or higher). They were not added to older versions. If your firmware is older, update it first. RecLink reports "FC too old for custom OSD" if it detects an older API.

1. Enable MSP on the UART#

  1. Connect the flight controller to Betaflight Configurator over USB and open the Ports tab.
  2. On the row for the UART you wired, switch on MSP in the Configuration/MSP column. Leave the baud rate at 115200.
  3. Click Save and Reboot.

Menu names can shift slightly between Configurator versions. The setting you need is "MSP on that UART at 115200 baud". If you must use another speed, set the same value in RecLink under Network → Baud rate (advanced mode) and reboot it.

2. Show the four custom message elements#

  1. Open the OSD tab.
  2. In the element list, tick Custom message 1, 2, 3 and 4 (until RecLink writes to them, Betaflight displays their placeholder names). Switch them on in the profile you fly.
  3. Drag each of the four into the layout. Each one is an independent element: put them where your goggles have room. A neat column in the left or right third works well.
  4. Save. Keep every line within 16 characters; that is the most Betaflight allows per message.
Placement tip

You only need to place the elements once. RecLink changes their text; Betaflight keeps the position. Keep them away from where you read battery voltage and warnings.

Analog or digital?

Pick your OSD type in the configurator's OSD tab. Digital (the default) is safe for DJI-style goggles; analog allows the full character set. See chapter 10.

3. Check your AUX channels#

RecLink reads the transmitter's channels through the flight controller. If you want a switch to start recording or select zones, assign it to a free AUX channel in your radio and confirm in Betaflight's Receiver tab that the bar moves. RecLink counts channels the same way Betaflight does: AUX 1 is channel 5. You do not need a Betaflight mode for these switches. In chapter 8 you will find the right channel by flipping the switch and letting RecLink detect it.

Chapter 5

First power-up and the web configurator#

Power the quad, join RecLink's Wi-Fi from your phone, and open the configurator. Everything from here on is done in that page.

  1. Remove the propellers, then power the flight controller from a battery or USB. The RecLink LED lights solid while it boots.
  2. Wait for the portal. When the board sees the flight controller and the aircraft is disarmed, it starts its own Wi-Fi network. The LED shows two short blinks every second while the portal is open.
  3. Join the network named RECLINK-XXXX (the last two bytes of the board's address) on your phone or laptop. The factory password is reclinkfpv.
  4. Open http://10.0.0.1 in a browser. Your phone may warn that the network has no internet. Stay connected. RecLink does not force a login sheet, so you open the address yourself.
  5. Change the Wi-Fi password when asked (below). Up to three devices can be connected at once.

Change the factory password#

While the password is still the factory default, the configurator shows this prompt each time you open it. Choose Yes, change it, type a new password of 8 to 31 characters and press Save & reboot. The board restarts and the Wi-Fi comes back with your new password. Tick Don't ask me anymore if you want to keep the default; the choice is stored on the board.

Write it down

If you forget the password you can still reach the portal by holding the button for 10 seconds to factory reset. That erases all settings and camera pairings.

When the portal opens and closes#

The portal never runs while the aircraft is armed.

  • Opens automatically when the aircraft is disarmed and the flight controller link is up. You can change this to "only after holding the button" in Network → Open the portal.
  • Closes after 15 minutes (adjustable from 1 to 120) once there has also been 60 seconds without activity, or when you hold the button for 3 seconds.
  • Never opens in flight. If the board reboots mid-flight, it waits until it knows the arm state before it will turn Wi-Fi on.

  • Saves are permanent. They are written to flash before the page confirms. Settings and camera pairings survive a reboot, a power loss and a firmware update.
  • Advanced mode (System tab) reveals timing fields, diagnostics and the Clock tab. Leave it off while you set up; turn it on when you need to fine-tune. It is remembered on the board.
  • Two settings need a reboot: the flight controller baud rate / pin swap, and Bluetooth power. After saving one of them a Reboot now button appears in the bar.
  • The portal is a bench tool. The page polls the board every 1.5 seconds. Keep one browser tab open, and close the portal before flying.

Chapter 6

Pairing your camera#

Pair once and RecLink reconnects by itself every time you power up. Pairing is done on the Binding tab.

GoPro#

  1. Open the Binding tab and press Pair GoPro.
  2. On the camera, swipe down, then go to Preferences → Connections → Connect Device → The Remote.
  3. When the camera shows that it is pairing, press Scan now in the portal. RecLink listens for up to 45 seconds. It does not connect to anything until you pick.
  4. Tap your camera in the list. If more than one GoPro is in pairing mode, check the name and signal strength first.
  5. A green check confirms the pairing. The camera appears under Paired cameras and its link goes to Connected.
If no camera shows up

The portal says "No GoPro found. Make sure it is in remote-pairing mode, then press Scan now again". Check that the camera is awake and showing its pairing screen. If it is connected to a phone app, disconnect it there first.

DJI Osmo#

  1. On the Binding tab press Pair DJI. The portal shows "pairing… put the DJI into pairing mode now".
  2. Put the Osmo into its pairing mode. On a first pairing the camera shows a confirmation prompt on its screen. Accept it.
  3. RecLink connects to the first matching DJI it hears. After this, reconnecting is automatic and needs no confirmation.

Managing paired cameras#

  • Up to four cameras, any mix of GoPro and DJI. Pairing a new one keeps the others.
  • One at a time. At power-up RecLink scans for every paired camera and connects to the strongest signal after about 2.5 seconds, or at once to one stronger than −50 dBm.
  • Badges: ● Connected · −52 dBm, Connecting…, or Standby for a paired camera that is not in use.
  • 3 s test recording starts the connected camera for three seconds. Use it to prove the whole chain works. It only runs while disarmed.
  • Forget removes one camera. Forget all cameras appears when two or more are paired and asks twice.
  • Telemetry poll (advanced mode) sets how often a GoPro is asked for status: Fast 0.5 s, Normal 1 s or Slow 1.5 s. Normal is right for most setups.
When a bond is lost

If the camera forgets RecLink (you reset its connections, or re-paired it to a phone), the next reconnects fail. After three failures RecLink forgets that camera's bond and raises a REPAIR_NEEDED flag on the Status tab. Pair the camera again.

Chapter 7

Status is read-only and tells you, at a glance, whether the chain flight controller → RecLink → camera is healthy.

Details

  • Flight controller: Betaflight variant and MSP API version. OSD grid is the canvas size the FC reports.
  • GPS: fix, satellites and whether the fix counts as good. Shown when the FC has a GPS.
  • Camera signal, time left, camera battery, SD card. SD shows free space in GB, or "NO SD" / "FULL".
  • Mode, resolution, profile as the camera reports them now.
  • GoPro Video and Protune values: ISO max/min, shutter angle (for example 180° (1/120)), Protune, white balance, colour, sharpness, EV comp, 10-bit.
  • Zone settings is the result of the last zone apply: Zone 1: applied, "partly applied (not accepted: …)", "not applied" or "waiting for camera".
A blank is honest

RecLink only shows what the camera confirmed. After a disconnect, every camera value goes blank until the camera answers again. That is deliberate: a stale or guessed number is worse than none.

With Advanced mode on, a Diagnostics card adds the live AUX channel values in µs, error counters and three collapsible debug panels (MSP link, Bluetooth connection, record trigger). They are useful for support, and for finding the stick position of a switch.

Chapter 8

Recording control#

Decide what starts and stops the camera. Everything here is on the Cam control tab, in the Recording card.

Choose a recording mode#

Example: the aircraft arms at 2, the switch goes on at 4, the aircraft disarms at 8 and the switch goes off at 10. Start and stop delays are left out.

ModeRecords whenGood for
Never (manual only)Never. You start the camera by hand.Bench work, or if you use the camera's own controls
When the aircraft is armedFrom arm to disarm.The simple default: every flight is recorded
From a radio switchWhile the switch is on, or toggled by a button.Choosing exactly when to film
Armed OR switch onArmed, or the switch is on. While armed, turning the switch off cuts the clip, and turning it on again starts a new one.Always film flights, but cut dead time
Armed AND switch onOnly when both are true. Releasing either one stops the clip at once.Film only when armed and you want it

Set up the radio switch#

  1. Pick From a radio switch, Armed OR switch on or Armed AND switch on. The Radio switch box becomes active. With Never or When the aircraft is armed it is greyed out and marked "not applicable".
  2. Find the channel. Press Set AUX, then flip the switch on your transmitter. The label changes to "push button or switch" while it listens. RecLink watches the live channel data, picks whichever channel moves by 200 µs or more, and fills in the AUX field. It gives up after 15 seconds. This needs the flight controller link to be up.
  3. Choose Trigger type. Switch mirrors the switch: on means recording, off means stop. Button is for a spring-loaded control: each press toggles recording and releasing does nothing.
  4. Check the threshold. The bar shows the control's live position as an amber line. The default of 1700 µs suits most 3-position switches (the top position counts as on). Adjust it by dragging the handle or typing a value from 900 to 2100.
  5. Press Save settings and try the switch. The Status tab shows "REC" and the logo dot turns red.
Prefer to look at the numbers?

Network → Scan AUX now lists the live value of every AUX channel. Move your switch and press it again to see which channel changed.

What happens when you trigger it#

Stopping works the same way after a stop delay (default 1.5 s), so the last moments are not clipped.

The camera is the judge: RecLink starts it, then waits for the camera to report it is recording. If it does not within the verify window, RecLink retries. If every try fails the OSD warning REC FAIL appears. The bridge tracks its own last command, so a camera that fails to report cannot cancel a stop RecLink owes it.

Chapter 9

Zones: camera settings from a switch#

A 2–6 position switch or knob on your radio picks a zone. Each zone stores the camera settings you want, and RecLink sends them to the camera when the stick rests in that zone.

A three-position switch split into three zones. The bar spans 900 to 2100 µs.

GoPro and DJI work differently

GoPro: a zone does not load a GoPro preset. It pushes the settings you ticked (resolution, frame rate, lens and so on) to the camera's current video preset. DJI: each zone applies a camera Mode ID number instead, typed into a field on the zone row.

Build the zones#

  1. Tick Enable zone control.
  2. Choose the AUX channel or press Set AUX and move the switch.
  3. Add or remove zones until the count matches your switch (3 for a 3-position switch). New edges are spread out by default: 1300 and 1700 µs for three zones.
  4. Move the stick through each position and check the bar's amber cursor lands in the right zone. Adjust the edges by dragging the handles. Edges stay in order and at least 25 µs apart. The last zone always ends at 2100 µs.
  5. Build the settings for each zone as shown on the next page.
Hold time

A zone must be held for 400 ms before it is applied, so a quick sweep through the middle does not fire every zone on the way. Change it under Advanced → Hold time before applying (50–5000 ms).

Camera settings for each zone (GoPro)#

Below the zone rows, one tab per zone shows a row for every setting a zone can change. Tick a row to push that setting; leave it unticked and the camera keeps its own value. Unticked rows are not stored.

Behaviour to know

  • The lists follow the camera. With the camera connected and in a video preset, each option list shows only what it accepts right now (for example, only the frame rates valid at the current resolution). A value the camera no longer offers stays visible, marked "(not available now)".
  • Order matters and is handled for you. RecLink writes resolution first, then the settings that depend on it, and Protune before its sub-settings. It waits for the camera to accept each write, checks the result, and retries a refused write once.
  • Shutter is a shutter angle. 180° means an exposure of 1/(2 × frame rate). The zone keeps the same angle if you change the frame rate.
  • Keep the camera in step while editing (on by default) means the camera really changes as you edit and as you select zone tabs. It works only while disarmed and not recording. Turn it off to edit without touching the camera.
  • Models differ. Aspect ratio, Bit depth and Color profile are hidden for a HERO11 because it does not have them. A HERO12 or 13 shows them.
GroupSettings a zone can push
VideoResolution, Aspect ratio, Frame rate, Lens, HyperSmooth, Bit depth, Color profile
ProtuneProtune on/off, ISO max, ISO min, Shutter angle, White balance, Color, Sharpness, EV comp, 10-bit

When a zone is applied#

  • RecLink applies a zone only when the camera link is ready, the camera is not busy, and it is not recording. To allow changes mid-recording, tick Allow zone changes while recording in the advanced options.
  • If a blocker is in the way it retries on the next tick, without losing the choice.
  • After a reconnect, the last selected zone is applied once, in case you changed the camera by hand.
  • The Status tab's Zone settings row tells you the result: applied, partly applied (and which settings the camera refused), or not applied. The camera, not RecLink, decides what it accepts. Some options are not valid with the current resolution.
  • A zone with no ticked settings pushes nothing.
Example

Zone 1 for cruising: 4K, 60 fps, Wide, HyperSmooth High. Zone 2 for a slow-motion pass: 2.7K, 120 fps, Linear. Zone 3 for max frame rate: 1080, 240 fps. Flip the switch in flight and the camera follows once the stick has rested for 400 ms.

Chapter 10

OSD: camera info in your goggles#

RecLink writes four lines of text, up to 16 characters each, into the four custom message elements you placed in chapter 4.

Each line is a template: normal text plus % tokens that RecLink replaces with live values. The defaults are shown here. Anything the camera has not confirmed is replaced by nothing at all.

Empty lines are sent as a single space so Betaflight does not show the placeholder name.

Edit the lines#

  1. Tap a line to select it (green border), then tap tokens in the palette to add them. Tap ＋ text to add literal text such as SD or BAT.
  2. Watch the preview. If a line would be longer than 16 characters the page warns "Line N is X chars, clipped to …". Shorten it.
  3. Prefer raw if you like typing templates directly, for example %st %et. Press Save settings.

When the camera is lost

If the camera link is not ready, line 1 reads CAM LOST, or a blinking CAM ? while RecLink is still searching or connecting. Lines 2–4 go blank. This layout always shows, whatever your warning settings say, so the OSD is never silently stale.

Chapter 11

Clock sync, GPS push and highlight tags#

Three smaller features that make your footage easier to sort and use.

Camera clock sync#

RecLink sets the camera's clock from the flight controller's GPS time, so every clip is stamped correctly, even after a battery swap that reset the camera to a default date. It writes to the camera only and never to the flight controller's clock.

It syncs when all of these are true

  • Clock sync is on, and (by default) the FC has a GPS with a steady 3D fix and at least 6 satellites for 10 seconds.
  • The FC reports a plausible time.
  • The camera is connected, not recording and not busy.

When it runs

  • When the camera connects, when the GPS fix becomes good, when you press Sync now, and then periodically (a GoPro every 5 minutes by default, a DJI every 60).
  • For a GoPro it first reads the camera clock and only writes if it is more than 10 seconds off, then checks the result. A DJI cannot be read, so its time is simply sent.

Set UTC offset in minutes (and Daylight saving) if you want footage in local time. Leave it at 0 for UTC.

If the clock cannot be fixed after the retries, the OSD shows CLK FAIL and the Clock tab reports FAILED. Only the timestamp is affected.

DJI GPS position push#

For a DJI camera only. On System → Send aircraft GPS position to the camera, RecLink streams the aircraft's GPS position to the Osmo at 1 or 2 Hz for the camera's own overlay or metadata. It needs a good GPS fix and valid time. A GoPro ignores this setting.

Highlight tag#

A spare switch can drop a marker into the footage at a moment you want to find later. Choose its AUX channel (use Set AUX). When the channel rises above 1700 µs, RecLink sends a HiLight tag to a GoPro. A DJI has no highlight tag, so RecLink presses its Quick-Switch button instead.

It works only while the camera link is ready. A momentary push-button is ideal.

Scan AUX now prints the current value of AUX 1–14 in µs. Move a switch and scan again to see which channel changed. It reads the same data as Set AUX, so it needs the flight controller link.

Advanced network settings

  • Radio power. Wi-Fi: Low 2 dBm, Medium 10 dBm, Max 20 dBm (default). Bluetooth: Low −12 dBm, Medium 0 dBm, Max +9 dBm (default). Raise power if the connection is unreliable, lower it to use less. Wi-Fi power applies the next time the portal opens. Bluetooth power applies after a reboot.
  • Baud rate (9600 to 460800, default 115200). Change this only if you changed the flight controller's UART speed, or to work around a marginal link. Both ends must match.
  • Swap TX/RX pins fixes crossed wires without soldering.
  • Both need a reboot. After saving, a Reboot now button appears in the bar. Nothing else here needs one.

Update the firmware (OTA)

  1. Get the firmware .bin file. Not the .zip.
  2. Under Danger → Firmware update, choose the file. Tick Erase all settings only if you want a clean start.
  3. Press Upload & flash and confirm. A progress bar shows the upload. Do not power off.
  4. The board reboots by itself and the page reloads. Settings and pairings are kept unless you chose to erase.

A bad image is rejected and the current firmware stays. The new firmware must start cleanly once before it is accepted, so a faulty update does not stick.

Factory reset

Erases all settings and all camera pairings, then reboots. Use the button on the System tab (it asks twice), or hold the board's button for 10 seconds. It is refused while the aircraft is armed. A normal reboot never clears anything.

Chapter 13

Flying with RecLink#

Once set up, RecLink does its job without you touching it. This chapter is the routine: check before, know what you are seeing during, and what happens if something drops.

Remember

RecLink 0.1.0 has not been flight tested. Bench-test every function with props off, as below, and keep your first flights short and over open ground.

What you see in the air#

  • Wi-Fi is off. Only Bluetooth runs, so the camera link is as stable as it can be.
  • The OSD text updates at its set rate. Recording state and elapsed time are always one glance away.
  • The LED is solid while recording.
  • Pressing the button in flight does nothing harmful.

If something drops

  • Camera link lost (out of range, camera off, radio dropout): OSD shows CAM LOST, the LED blinks fast, and RecLink keeps trying with growing pauses (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, then every 30 seconds). When it reconnects it checks what the camera is doing and sends at most one corrective command.
  • Flight controller link lost: RecLink stops writing text. The aircraft is unaffected.
  • RecLink restarts (brown-out or stall): the OSD text freezes for about 0.3 seconds, then recovers. The camera reconnects by itself.

The camera link lifecycle. SCAN, CONNECTING and SYNCING show as one searching state.

Wi-Fi and the configurator#

ProblemTry
I cannot see the RECLINK networkThe portal only runs while disarmed and with the flight controller link up. Check the aircraft is disarmed and the LED shows two quick blinks per second. My changes disappearedPress Save settings. Changes apply and persist only after saving. The bar says "unsaved changes" until you do.

Flight controller and OSD#

ProblemTry
Status shows FC offlineCheck wiring: FC TX to RecLink RX and FC RX to RecLink TX. Check MSP is enabled on that UART at 115200. Try Swap TX/RX pins (advanced), save and reboot. If you changed the baud, match it on both sides.
OSD shows nothing from RecLinkConfirm Betaflight is 2025.12 or newer, and that Custom message 1–4 are enabled and placed in the OSD tab. An OSD_UNSUPPORTED chip means the API is too old.
Placeholder names instead of textThe line is empty or RecLink is not connected yet. Check the FC link first.
OSD symbols look wrongMatch OSD type to your goggles. Digital filters to plain letters and digits. Use analog only for an analog OSD chip.
A line is cut offLines are limited to 16 characters. Shorten the template. The preview warns you.
The board resets in flightCheck the 5 V supply. Add a 220 µF or larger capacitor across the board's power pads. Watch for WDT_RECOVERED or a rising reboot counter in Diagnostics.

Camera#

ProblemTry
The camera is not found when pairingGoPro: put it in Connections → Connect Device → The Remote first, then press Scan now. Disconnect it from any phone app. Move RecLink closer. Make sure fewer than four cameras are already paired.
"Bond list full"Four cameras are the limit. Forget one on the Binding tab.
REPAIR_NEEDED chip, or the camera never reconnectsThe camera forgot RecLink, usually after its connections were reset. Forget it on the Binding tab, then pair it again.
It connects, then drops again and againMove the board away from carbon and battery leads. In Network (advanced) raise Bluetooth power to Max and reboot. After three silent hangs in two minutes RecLink reboots itself to clear the link.

Status LED#

What a single-colour LED does over a six-second window.

Timings are nominal. On the production board the LED is a single-colour LED, so patterns differ by rhythm only. Boards with an addressable LED use colour too (blue for the portal, green for recording). The LED shows the highest-priority pattern that applies: portal, then a recent watchdog recovery, then recording, then flight controller down, then the camera link state. Identify overrides everything while it runs.

What RecLink can and cannot touch#

It reads

  • Flight controller: API version, craft name, arm state, RC channels, GPS, battery state, clock.
  • Camera: recording, battery, SD, mode, resolution, frame rate and the other values shown on the Status tab.

It writes

  • Four lines of OSD text to the flight controller.
  • Record, settings, highlight and clock commands to the camera.

It never sends

  • Any RC, GPS, clock, display-port or reboot command to the flight controller.
  • Any command that arms or disarms.
  • Any formatting, firmware, reset or file-delete command to the camera from automation. Those exist only as labelled portal actions, while disarmed.
