RecLink hardware

The board

The custom RecLink PCB: an ESP32-C3 with its own antenna, about the size of a fingernail, with four solder pads to the flight controller.

In development

The RecLink board is currently in development. What you see here is the work-in-progress design: dimensions, pad layout and components may still change, and it is not a finished product yet. Until then, any ESP32-C3 or ESP32-S3 dev board works (see the Quick start).

Scroll to take the board apart. Drag to look around.

  1. The assembled board12 × 13 mm, one ESP32-C3 and a handful of tiny parts. Keep scrolling to take it apart.
  2. ComponentsThe ESP32-C3 chip, ceramic antenna, crystal, status LED and about twenty tiny resistors and capacitors.
  3. Top layerSolder mask, exposed pads, silkscreen and the first copper layer.
  4. Inner layersTwo copper layers hidden inside the board give the routing room it needs on such a small board.
  5. Bottom layerThe bottom side carries the four solder pads and the test points.
  6. Four wiresGND, 5V, TX and RX are all that connects the board to the flight controller.

The four pads

Solder four wires from the flight controller to the four pads along the board's edge, in the order printed on its bottom side (left to right when you look at that side):

PadConnect toNotes
GNDFC groundCommon ground.
5VFC 5VPower. The board regulates it down on its own.
TXFC UART RXBoard transmit (GPIO21, through a 470 Ω series resistor).
RXFC UART TXBoard receive (GPIO20).

TX and RX are named from the board's side, so TX goes to the flight controller's RX. If you cross them by mistake, you can fix it in the web configurator (MSP tab, Swap MSP TX/RX pins) without re-soldering.
